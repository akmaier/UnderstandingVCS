This supplement collects the analyses behind the main paper. We give
the full proofs of forward equivalence, the temperature-limit bound,
and finite-parameter exactness; a numerical study of how $\alpha$
and $T$ shape the gradient; measurements of forward bit-exactness
under relaxation; throughput and active development time; and
beam-timed comparison results. We first restate the execution
modes and prove their properties.

\section{Setup and Notation}

The full VCS state is
$x=(\text{CPU registers},\text{RAM},\text{RIOT},\text{TIA},
\text{cartridge},\text{frame buffer})$.
The hard transition $\Phi_{\mathrm{H}}$ executes one CPU instruction
on the complete bit-exact machine. The full-system SOFT-STE
transition $\Phi_{\mathrm{S}}$ first executes this hard instruction,
including all bus and device effects. It then replays the instruction
through differentiable CPU/RAM handlers against the same hard machine.
ROM bytes are read through the current bank/slice mapping of the
hard cartridge. TIA, RIOT, and Superchip register reads are
replayed as constants from the hard bus; writes outside RAM
affect only the hard devices.

Every CPU register and RAM cell is returned as
$\mathrm{STE}(\text{soft},\text{hard})$. TIA, RIOT, cartridge,
and frame-buffer states are carried by the hard machine and
receive no gradient. The backward pass reaches CPU registers,
RAM, and ROM bytes. We define five primitives for the
differentiable handlers and relaxed execution.

The first primitive is the memory read. A read of a memory tensor
$r\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$ of size $M$ (the ROM or the RAM) at an integer address
$a\in\{0,\dots,M-1\}$ is written as a dot product against the one-hot address
indicator $\mathbf{1}_a$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{peek}(r,a)=\mathbf{1}_a^{\top}r=\textstyle\sum_i[\![i=a]\!]\,r_i=r_a ,
\label{eq:s-peek}
\end{equation}
so the forward value is the array element $r_a$, while the gradient with respect
to $r$ is the one-hot vector $\mathbf{1}_a$.

The second primitive is opcode dispatch. With a score vector (logits)
$\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ over the $K=256$ opcodes, a temperature $T>0$, and the
softmax weights $w=\softmax(\ell/T)$, the relaxed dispatch over the candidate
handler-output rows $V$ is the convex combination
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{select}(\ell,V;T)=w^{\top}V,\qquad
w_k=\frac{e^{\ell_k/T}}{\sum_j e^{\ell_j/T}} .
\label{eq:s-select}
\end{equation}
This is a definition of a possible relaxation, not an executed
dispatch mode. Every mode studied here uses the hard switch
on the decoded opcode byte. No executed mode relaxes opcode
dispatch, so it contributes no error term to the bounds below.

The third primitive is the conditional branch. For a relaxed status flag
$f\in[0,1]$ written as a logit $z=s(2f-1)$, where the sign $s$ selects
branch-when-set or branch-when-clear, and a sharpness $\alpha$, the gate and the
relaxed program counter are
\begin{equation}
g=\sigma(\alpha z),\qquad
\mathrm{pc}_{\mathrm{soft}}=(1-g)\,\mathrm{pc}_{\bar b}+g\,\mathrm{pc}_{b}
=\mathrm{pc}_{\bar b}+g\,\delta ,
\label{eq:s-branch}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{pc}_{\bar b}$ is the fall-through (not-taken) counter and
$\mathrm{pc}_{b}=\mathrm{pc}_{\bar b}+\delta$ is the taken counter for the signed
branch offset $\delta$.

The fourth primitive is the straight-through estimator. For any soft value that
has a matching hard value it returns
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{STE}(\text{soft},\text{hard})=\text{soft}+\sg(\text{hard}-\text{soft}),
\label{eq:s-ste}
\end{equation}
where $\sg(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator, so the forward value equals
\textit{hard} while the backward pass uses the gradient of \textit{soft}.

The fifth primitive is the \emph{relaxed read}, the differentiable counterpart of
the exact read~\eqref{eq:s-peek}. It blurs the integer address with a
distance-softmax of width $T$, returning the temperature-weighted average of the
neighbouring cells,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{R}}(r,a;T)=\textstyle\sum_k w_k\,r_{a+k},\qquad
w_k=\frac{e^{-|k|/T}}{\sum_j e^{-|j|/T}},
\label{eq:s-read}
\end{equation}
so that $\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{R}}\!\to r_a$ as $T\to0$ while the address carries
a nonzero gradient for $T>0$. The executed SOFT-STE path uses the exact one-hot
read~\eqref{eq:s-peek}; FULL and LEAKY use~\eqref{eq:s-read}
with their results rounded back to integers.

Table~\ref{tab:s-modes} summarises the six execution
and gradient modes. FULL relaxes branches and reads only, with
hard opcode dispatch. LEAKY is the same construction just
outside the sufficient exactness region. NAIVE differentiates
hard execution through discrete indices; SAMPLER adds a
bilinear position kernel while retaining the hard forward.

\begin{assumption}[Decision margins]
\label{ass:s-dm}
Along the executed trajectory of length $N$, each
relaxed conditional branch has strictly positive
flag-logit margin $m_t=|z_t|>0$.
Let $m_{\min}=\min_t m_t>0$.
\end{assumption}

For exact processor flags, $z_t=\pm1$ and $m_{\min}=1$.
The relaxed read~\eqref{eq:s-read} needs no additional
margin assumption: the addressed integer byte is
separated from other addresses by distance at least one.
Opcode dispatch is hard.

\paragraph{Numerical scope.}
IEEE-754 float32 represents every integer in
$[-2^{24},2^{24}]$ exactly
\citep{ieee754_2019,goldberg1991}. All VCS register
values, data bytes, addresses, and instruction-local
integer quantities lie within this range.
The full-system SOFT-STE forward uses hard-machine
values for every state component, so it does not
accumulate soft arithmetic or rounding error.
The exactness statement concerns these returned
values; the soft computation supplies gradients.

\section{Proofs}

\begin{theorem}[Exact forward equivalence]
\label{thm:s-exact}
For every reachable full-machine state $x$ and finite
sharpness $\alpha$ and temperature $T>0$, the full-system
SOFT-STE step equals the hard step:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathrm{S}}(x)=\Phi_{\mathrm{H}}(x).
\label{eq:s-onestep}
\end{equation}
The complete trajectories therefore agree for every
instruction under identical initial states and inputs.
The modes differ only in their backward rules.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first prove the one-step equality for an arbitrary
reachable state $x$, then extend it by induction.

\textit{Hard instruction and soft replay.}
The full-system step first executes the instruction on
the bit-exact hard machine. This updates the CPU
registers, RAM, bus, TIA, RIOT, cartridge, and
frame buffer exactly as $\Phi_{\mathrm{H}}$ does.
The selected opcode handler is determined by the
hard decoded byte.

The soft CPU/RAM handlers then replay the instruction
against that same hard machine. Opcode dispatch
remains the hard switch. A ROM read uses the current
hard-cartridge bank/slice mapping; a RAM read uses
the soft tape. Reads of TIA, RIOT, and Superchip
registers are constants from the hard bus, and
writes outside RAM go to hard devices only.
These rules ensure that the soft replay does not
replace the hard device state with an approximation.

\textit{STE join.}
Let $h_j$ be the hard result for any CPU register
or RAM cell and $s_j$ its soft replay result.
The returned value is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{STE}(s_j,h_j)
&=s_j+\sg(h_j-s_j)\\
&=h_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-branch-eq}
\end{equation}
This equality holds for every value of $s_j$, without
assuming that the soft handler numerically reproduces
the hard one. Thus every returned CPU register and
RAM cell is exactly hard. All other state components
are already carried by the hard machine. Therefore
every component of the complete state agrees, proving
$\Phi_{\mathrm{S}}(x)=\Phi_{\mathrm{H}}(x)$.

\textit{Floating-point equality.}
The hard values of VCS bytes, registers, addresses,
and instruction-local counts lie within the exact
integer range of float32. IEEE-754 single precision
has a 24-bit significand and represents all integers
in $[-2^{24},2^{24}]$ exactly
\citep{ieee754_2019,goldberg1991}.
For example, the one-hot read obeys
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{S}}(r,a)
=\mathbf{1}_a^{\top}r=r_a
=\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{H}}(r,a).
\label{eq:s-peek-eq}
\end{equation}
The STE join likewise returns the hard integer value.
Hence no representation error is introduced in the
returned state. Equality is exact, not a tolerance
comparison.

\textit{Induction on the trajectory.}
Let the two runs share $x_0$ and the same input
sequence. For the base case,
$x^{\mathrm{S}}_0=x^{\mathrm{H}}_0$.
If $x^{\mathrm{S}}_t=x^{\mathrm{H}}_t=:x$,
one-step equality gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
x^{\mathrm{S}}_{t+1}
&=\Phi_{\mathrm{S}}(x)\\
&=\Phi_{\mathrm{H}}(x)
=x^{\mathrm{H}}_{t+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By induction, the complete trajectories agree at
every instruction.

\textit{Gradients.}
The stop-gradient changes the reverse-mode rule
without changing the forward result. For example,
the relaxed branch gate contributes
$\alpha g(1-g)\delta$ to the derivative with
respect to its logit, where the corresponding
hard branch is discrete. The memory
read~\eqref{eq:s-peek} supplies the one-hot
Jacobian $\mathbf{1}_a$ with respect to memory
contents. The soft replay therefore exposes
surrogate gradients through CPU registers, RAM,
and ROM bytes. TIA, RIOT, cartridge, and
frame-buffer states carry no gradient.
These derivatives are not the derivatives of
the hard discrete transition.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Measured handler agreement.}
We tested the full-system soft step in both ports.
In \textsc{jutari}, all 64 ROMs were checked
starting 20 frames into play, over 3,000 instructions
per game. SOFT-STE equalled HARD at every instruction
(64/64), and the soft handlers before STE correction
reproduced every hard CPU register and RAM cell,
with zero disagreements.

In \textsc{jaxtari}, nine ROMs cover every mapper
family exercised by the conformance set:
Pong and Breakout (2K), Space Invaders (4K),
Ms.\ Pac-Man and Up'n Down (F8), Double Dunk (F6),
Elevator Action (F8SC), Montezuma's Revenge (E0),
and Robot Tank (FE). After standard boot and
20 frames of play, SOFT-STE equalled the hard
console on all 1,500 instructions per ROM,
with zero soft-handler disagreements.
Furthermore, \texttt{jax.grad} through 100
full-system steps reaches ROM bytes.

The \textsc{jutari} check exposed two soft-handler
defects, both fixed. The functional \texttt{BRK}
handler did not push the required state and jump
through the IRQ vector. Instruction fetches also
always read ROM, incorrectly handling code executed
from RAM, as in Up'n Down at
$\mathrm{pc}=\texttt{\$00EA}$.
The tests therefore check the computation through
which gradients pass, not only the STE result.

\begin{theorem}[Temperature-limit bound]
\label{thm:s-limit}
Consider FULL, which uses sigmoid
branches~\eqref{eq:s-branch} and distance-softmax
reads~\eqref{eq:s-read} without STE correction,
while retaining hard opcode dispatch.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:s-dm}, the deviations
of the unrounded relaxed branch and read values
from their hard counterparts satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\big\|\Phi_{\mathrm{S}}^{T,\alpha}(x)
-\Phi_{\mathrm{H}}(x)\big\|_\infty
\le C\big[
e^{-\alpha m_{\min}}
+\rho e^{-1/T}\big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-bound}
\end{equation}
where the norm denotes the local deviations of
these relaxed operations, $C$ bounds their
value ranges and fixed geometric factors, and
$\rho$ bounds the number of reads per step.
The branch and read errors converge separately
as $\alpha\to\infty$ and $T\to0$:
\[
\mathcal{O}(e^{-\alpha m_{\min}}+e^{-1/T}).
\]
No opcode-dispatch term occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We bound the two relaxed operations evaluated
on the same hard input state. The bound concerns
their unrounded values; rounding is addressed
separately in Proposition~\ref{prop:s-finite}.

\textit{Branch term.}
For a branch with margin $m=|z|\ge m_{\min}$,
the difference between the sigmoid gate and the
hard decision is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\big|\sigma(\alpha z)-[\![z>0]\!]\big|
&=\sigma(-\alpha m)\\
&\le e^{-\alpha m_{\min}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-gate}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the unrounded program counter
differs from the hard target by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\big|(g-[\![z>0]\!])\delta\big|
&\le|\delta|e^{-\alpha m_{\min}}\\
&\le C e^{-\alpha m_{\min}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-branchterm}
\end{equation}

\textit{Read term.}
For an addressed byte $r_a$, the
distance-softmax read gives
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{R}}(r,a;T)-r_a
=\sum_{k\ne0}w_k(r_{a+k}-r_a).
\end{equation}
Let $w_0$ denote the weight of the addressed
byte. Since bytes lie in $[0,255]$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\big|\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{R}}(r,a;T)-r_a\big|
&\le255(1-w_0).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The denominator in~\eqref{eq:s-read} contains
the addressed weight $1$, so
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
1-w_0
&\le2\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}e^{-d/T}\\
&=\frac{2e^{-1/T}}{1-e^{-1/T}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-readmass}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
\big|\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{R}}(r,a;T)-r_a\big|
\le\frac{510e^{-1/T}}{1-e^{-1/T}}.
\label{eq:s-readterm}
\end{equation}
For $T$ bounded above by any fixed $T_0>0$,
the denominator is bounded away from zero.
Absorbing this fixed factor and the byte range
into $C$ gives the rate $C e^{-1/T}$.
The $\rho$ reads contribute at most
$\rho C e^{-1/T}$.

\textit{Combined bound.}
The executed opcode dispatch is a hard switch;
its contribution is exactly zero. Adding the
branch and read contributions yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\mathrm{step}}
&\le C\big[
e^{-\alpha m_{\min}}
+\rho e^{-1/T}\big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-perstep}
\end{equation}
which is~\eqref{eq:s-bound}. The two
exponential rates remain separate; no relation
between $\alpha$ and $T$ is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-parameter exactness]
\label{prop:s-finite}
FULL retains hard opcode dispatch and rounds
each relaxed branch target and memory-read result
to an integer. If every relaxed value differs
from the corresponding hard integer by less
than $1/2$, the rounded value equals the hard
one. Hence the entire rounded trajectory is
bit-exact.

For a binary-flag branch with offset $\delta$,
the sufficient and, away from rounding ties,
necessary condition is
\begin{equation}
|\delta|\sigma(-\alpha)<\frac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\delta|<\frac{1+e^\alpha}{2}.
\label{eq:s-branchcorner}
\end{equation}
Since $|\delta|\le128$ for a 6502 branch,
$\alpha\ge6$ suffices.

For an 8-bit distance-softmax read,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
|\mathrm{peek}_{\mathrm{R}}(r,a;T)-r_a|
&\le255(1-w_0)\\
&\le\frac{510e^{-1/T}}{1-e^{-1/T}}\\
&<\frac12 \qquad(T\le0.14).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-readcorner}
\end{equation}
Thus $\alpha\ge6$, $T\le0.14$ are
program-independent sufficient conditions
for the rounded FULL trajectory to equal HARD.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textit{Branch casts.}
A hard branch with a binary flag chooses either
$\mathrm{pc}_{\bar b}$ or
$\mathrm{pc}_{b}=\mathrm{pc}_{\bar b}+\delta$.
The sigmoid gate leaves weight
$\sigma(-\alpha)$ on the unselected target.
Therefore the absolute displacement of the
relaxed result from the hard target is exactly
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_b=|\delta|\sigma(-\alpha).
\end{equation}
Rounding to the nearest integer returns the
hard target whenever $\epsilon_b<1/2$.
Using
$\sigma(-\alpha)=1/(1+e^\alpha)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_b<\frac12
&\Longleftrightarrow
\frac{|\delta|}{1+e^\alpha}<\frac12\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
|\delta|<\frac{1+e^\alpha}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At $\alpha=6$, the right-hand side is
approximately $202.2$, exceeding the maximum
6502 offset magnitude of $128$. Hence all
binary-flag branches round to their hard target.

\textit{Memory-read casts.}
For address $a$, let $w_0$ be the weight of
the addressed byte. Each other byte differs
from $r_a$ by at most $255$, so
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_r
&=\left|\sum_{k\ne0}w_k(r_{a+k}-r_a)\right|\\
&\le255\sum_{k\ne0}w_k
=255(1-w_0).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For each distance $d\ge1$, at most two
addresses lie at that distance from $a$.
Furthermore, the normalising denominator is
at least one. Thus
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
1-w_0
&\le2\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}e^{-d/T}\\
&=\frac{2e^{-1/T}}{1-e^{-1/T}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Reads near either end of the memory have fewer
neighbours, which can only decrease this bound.
It follows that
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_r
\le\frac{510e^{-1/T}}{1-e^{-1/T}}.
\end{equation}
At $T=0.14$, the right-hand side is
approximately $0.40<1/2$. The worst-case
threshold is approximately $T=0.1443$.
Hence every relaxed byte read rounds exactly
to its addressed hard value for $T\le0.14$,
regardless of the program or byte contents.

\textit{Induction over instructions.}
At the initial instruction the FULL and HARD
states agree. Suppose they agree immediately
before some instruction. Both decode the same
opcode through the hard dispatch switch and
execute the same non-relaxed operations.
By the preceding bounds, every relaxed branch
target and memory-read result rounds to its
hard counterpart. Therefore the state after
the instruction also agrees. Induction proves
equality for the entire trajectory, without
a Lipschitz or error-propagation assumption.
\end{proof}

The bounds are sufficient, not necessary:
outside this corner, exactness depends on the
branch offsets and byte patterns actually
encountered. At $\alpha=6$, $T=0.14$, FULL
was measured bit-exact for 6,000 instructions
on Pong, Breakout, and Space Invaders.
LEAKY ($\alpha=5.5$, $T=0.145$) lies just
outside the sufficient region and exhibits
mild forward divergence in the measured scene.

\section{Effect of the Relaxation Parameters on the Gradient}

Theorems~\ref{thm:s-exact} and~\ref{thm:s-limit} show
that exact forward execution and useful gradients
are distinct properties. We illustrate how $\alpha$
and $T$ affect surrogate gradients in \textsc{jutari},
using a target invader sprite whose horizontal placement
is controlled by the soft primitives.

For the sigmoid branch, we plot the screen-space
directional derivative
$\partial(\text{screen})/\partial(\text{control})$
while varying $\alpha$ (Figure~\ref{fig:alphatemp}).
The colour encodes the sign of the derivative:
red where a pixel brightens as the control increases,
blue where it darkens. Intensity denotes magnitude.
The colour bar gives absolute units; each panel
also reports its peak relative to the row maximum.

For a position
$\mathrm{pc}=(1-g)\mathrm{pc}_L+g\mathrm{pc}_R$,
with $g=\sigma(\alpha z)$, the derivative is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathrm{pc}}{\partial z}
=\alpha g(1-g)(\mathrm{pc}_R-\mathrm{pc}_L).
\end{equation}
This produces a dipole at the two sprite placements.
Its magnitude is non-monotonic in $\alpha$ at fixed
$z=0.25$: small $\alpha$ gives a shallow gate,
while large $\alpha$ saturates it. The measured
relative peaks for $\alpha=2,6,20$ are
$0.53,1.00,0.15$, respectively. The intermediate
$\alpha=6$ gives the strongest gradient here.

The forward render in this STE gradient experiment
remains the exact hard one (Theorem~\ref{thm:s-exact});
only the surrogate derivative changes. The gradient
vanishes as the relaxation approaches its hard limit.
This is a property of the estimator, not evidence
that its attributions are faithful.

\section{Forward Bit-Exactness Under Full Relaxation}

SOFT-STE is forward-exact independently of $\alpha$
and $T$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:s-exact}). FULL instead
relaxes conditional branches and memory reads without
STE correction, retaining hard opcode dispatch.
Every relaxed branch target and byte read is rounded
back to an integer. Proposition~\ref{prop:s-finite}
proves that $\alpha\ge6$ and $T\le0.14$ suffice for
exactness independently of the executed program.

We test the rounding boundaries on the full
\textsc{jutari} simulator executing Space Invaders.
The hard, cycle-timed TIA and other devices remain
unchanged; only the CPU's branch and read operations
pass through the relaxation hook.

\centering

\label{fig:relaxheat}
\end{figure}

A crisp-flag branch with signed offset $\delta$
rounds to the hard target when
$|\delta|<\tau_b(\alpha)$, where
$\tau_b(\alpha)=\tfrac12(1+e^\alpha)$.
The empirical per-branch exactness
$p_{\mathrm{branch}}(\alpha)$ is the fraction of
executed offsets satisfying this condition.

A relaxed read is exact when its neighbour pull
\begin{equation}
\left|\sum_{k\ne0}
w_k\big(\mathrm{mem}[a+k]-\mathrm{mem}[a]\big)\right|
<\frac12 .
\end{equation}
The corresponding empirical fraction is
$p_{\mathrm{read}}(T)$. Treating individual casts
as independent gives the heuristic per-step
likelihood
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{step}}(\alpha,T)
=p_{\mathrm{read}}(T)^\rho
p_{\mathrm{branch}}(\alpha)^{f_b}.
\label{eq:s-castlikelihood}
\end{equation}
For Space Invaders, the executed trace has
$\rho=2.16$ reads per instruction and branch
fraction $f_b=0.374$. Independence is an
approximation, so $P_{\mathrm{step}}$ is not a
probabilistic guarantee. Exactness of a particular
trajectory requires every cast to round correctly;
Proposition~\ref{prop:s-finite} supplies sufficient
conditions for this without the likelihood model.

\paragraph{Choosing $\alpha$ and $T$.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:s-finite} gives
program-independent sufficient conditions.
For 6502 branch offsets $|\delta|\le128$,
$\alpha=6$ gives
$\tfrac12(1+e^\alpha)\approx202.2>128$.
For byte reads, the worst-case pull
$510e^{-1/T}/(1-e^{-1/T})$ is approximately
$0.40$ at $T=0.14$, below the rounding
threshold $1/2$. The exact sufficient
temperature threshold is approximately $0.1443$.
Thus $\alpha=6,T=0.14$ guarantees
bit-exact rounded branch and read operations
under the stated model.

Measurements confirm bit-exact execution over
6,000 instructions on Pong, Breakout, and
Space Invaders at this setting. Outside the
sufficient corner, failure depends on the
executed program: $\alpha=5$ diverges on
Space Invaders but not Pong or Breakout;
$T=0.15$ diverges on Pong and Space Invaders
but not Breakout. The selected setting lies
near the finite-parameter boundary, retaining
a useful relaxation gradient while preserving
the exact forward. More extreme parameters
saturate the branch and read gradients.

\paragraph{Full-simulator demonstration.}
Figure~\ref{fig:divframe} compares four
beam-timed Space Invaders rollouts over 60 seconds.
HARD, SOFT-STE, and FULL at $\alpha=6,T=0.14$
render identical frames. The relaxed executions
use the same cycle-accurate hard renderer with
a default-off relaxation hook for CPU branches
and reads. LEAKY at $\alpha=5.5,T=0.145$
diverges mildly but remains recognisable,
with a persistent missing invader column
caused by a corrupted sprite-position read.

\paragraph{Delayed divergence.}
A relaxed run can boot exactly and diverge only
after the game state changes. RAM neighbour
values evolve, and a read may cross its
rounding threshold only after many frames.
For a clean-boot rollout with relaxation
enabled afterward and $\alpha=20$,
the first divergent frame is $1$ at
$T=0.1448$, $194$ at $T=0.1446$, and
$2365$ (approximately 40 seconds) at
$T\le0.1444$. Below $T=0.1465$, the
lowest fixed ROM-read threshold, opcode and
operand fetches remain exact; these late
failures come from evolving RAM reads.
The boundary is therefore a spectrum of
fixed ROM and state-dependent RAM thresholds.

\section{Per-Game Conformance}

Table~\ref{tab:games} lists every game in the conformance set with its
auto-detected mapper, a short ROM hash, and its per-frame RAM and pixel
exactness, so the headline 64/64 claim can be audited game by game. Both ports
pass every game; \textsc{jaxtari} and \textsc{jutari} agree game-for-game, and
the cross-check between them is part of the test suite.

The conformance protocol uses the standard ALE boot
(60 NOOP frames and four resets), followed by a
fixed action stream. RAM is checked on every one
of 30 frames and the complete $210\times160$
frame buffer on every one of 60 frames.
The results establish exactness on these tested
trajectories, not on all possible executions.
Ten cartridge mappers are implemented, seven
exercised by the 64 games.

The \textsc{xitari} reference originally seeds
Superchip RAM with \texttt{time(NULL)}, irrespective
of its emulator seed. We patched this initialisation
to zero to match the deterministic ports.
The patch ships with the code.

\section{Throughput}

One step is one CPU instruction, not an ALE
frame. We distinguish the soft CPU core, used
for the batched \textsc{jaxtari} measurements,
from the full-system SOFT-STE step.

The soft CPU core has a simplified bus:
no TIA, a single 4\,KB ROM window, and only
the RIOT timer. It excludes the complete TIA,
RIOT, and cartridge execution of the full-system
step. The \textsc{jaxtari} hard interpreter
executes eagerly and cannot be batched on GPU.
Consequently the GPU throughput figures do not
measure full-machine differentiable rollouts.

CPU measurements use an Apple M1 Max core
(Julia 1.12, JAX CPU backend). GPU measurements
use a GTX 1080 Ti and a Quadro RTX 5000.
JIT warm-up is excluded. Table~\ref{tab:s-compare}
summarises soft-core rates; Tables~\ref{tab:s-batched}
and~\ref{tab:s-gpu} give batch scaling.

\paragraph{Soft CPU core.}
At single-instruction granularity,
\textsc{jutari} reaches approximately 370,100
instructions/s and \textsc{jaxtari} 1,178.
JAX incurs substantial per-operation overhead
at this granularity. Compiling a 3,000-instruction
Pong rollout into \texttt{lax.scan} increases
single-environment \textsc{jaxtari} throughput
to approximately 30,000 instructions/s.
A 50,000-instruction trace takes approximately
42 seconds for one forward-plus-backward pass
(gradient) through the \textsc{jaxtari} soft CPU
core with its simplified bus, as used for one
attribution. This is not the full-system step.

Batching with \texttt{vmap} increases aggregate
CPU throughput toward 60,000 instructions/s.
The 256-way \texttt{lax.switch} evaluates candidate
branches under batching, limiting CPU scaling
but allowing effective parallel evaluation
on GPU.

\paragraph{GPU.}
On the GTX 1080 Ti, forward throughput rises
from approximately 2,500 instructions/s at
$N=1$ to 2.95 million at $N=4096$,
approximately 50 times the CPU batching limit,
then declines slightly at larger batches.
Forward+gradient reaches approximately
2.80 million instructions/s at the peak.
The workload uses approximately 0.1\,GB there,
so available parallelism rather than memory
capacity limits performance.

The Quadro RTX 5000 shows the same scaling
pattern, peaking at
$3{,}117{,}908\pm2{,}871$ instructions/s
(mean and standard deviation over 10 runs)
near $N=4096$.
The GPU results establish efficient batched
soft CPU core execution, not differentiable
full-machine rendering.

\paragraph{Full-system soft step.}
Both ports implement the full-system SOFT-STE
construction: the hard machine executes CPU,
bus, TIA, RIOT, and cartridge effects; soft
handlers replay the instruction; CPU registers
and RAM are joined with STE. In \textsc{jutari},
the step reaches approximately 146,000
instructions/s on Pong using one Apple M1 Max
core (median of three runs). In \textsc{jaxtari},
it executes eagerly on CPU and is slow;
its measured GPU throughput applies only
to the simplified soft CPU core.

Zygote reverse-mode gradients through this
full-system step depend strongly on window length.
Measured throughput is approximately
400--450 instructions/s over 50--100-instruction
windows, 250 instructions/s at 200 instructions,
and 120 instructions/s at 400 instructions.
It falls further for longer windows.
Both the full-system and older soft-core
reverse passes slow down superlinearly with
window length under Zygote. The soft core
is approximately 1.5 times faster at 50
instructions, 3.7 times at 100, 4.8 times
at 200, and 7 times at 400 instructions.

Thus full-system gradients are practical for
short windows, but not for million-step
differentiable rollouts. An efficient per-step
reverse pass is future work.

\section{Active Development Time}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} gives the version-control
evidence for the active development time estimate.
All figures derive from the project's \texttt{git}
history, which will be released with the code.
Commit timestamps provide a wall-time proxy,
not a direct measurement of person-effort,
coding-agent compute, or total labour.

We group commits into sessions separated by
gaps greater than three hours. This exceeds
the median in-session gap of approximately
13 minutes. A two-hour cutoff gives 106 hours;
a four-hour cutoff gives 162 hours, compared
with 136.8 hours at three hours.

The 373 commits form 52 sessions across 29
calendar days, totalling approximately 5.7
round-the-clock days of active development
wall-time. Around 80\% of the calendar span
is excluded as idle, including travel without
reliable internet access. The figure is an
order-of-magnitude estimate, not a controlled
comparison with conventional development.
